# The Riemann Hypothesis

## Problem Statement

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH), formulated by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, states that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part equal to 1/2.

For complex numbers satisfying:

\[
\Re(s) > 1
\]

the zeta function is defined as

\[
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^s}
\]

and extends analytically to the entire complex plane except for a simple pole at \(s=1\).

The hypothesis asserts:

\[
\zeta(\rho)=0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\Re(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}
\]

for every non-trivial zero \(\rho\).

---

## Importance

The Riemann Hypothesis is central to:

- Prime number distribution
- Analytic number theory
- Arithmetic geometry
- Random matrix theory
- Quantum chaos
- Cryptography

Many existing theorems are conditional upon RH.
